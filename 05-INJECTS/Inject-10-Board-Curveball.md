## Format

The board session is 90 minutes: 60 presenting, 30 questions. Inject 10 occupies
part of the question period.

At a point of the mentor's choosing, a board member introduces a fact, a
question or a development that the pack does not cover. The teams respond live.
There is no preparation time and no opportunity to go away and come back.

Between two and four are used, depending on how the session is running. They
escalate: the first is answerable from work already done, the last is not
answerable at all and tests what the team does when that is true.

## What is being assessed

Not whether the answer is right. Several have no right answer.

| Dimension | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Holding a position** | Defends a sound conclusion under pressure from someone senior, and does not retreat because the questioner is displeased |
| **Changing a position** | Changes it when given genuinely new information, says so explicitly, and says what it changes |
| **The boundary of knowledge** | Says "we did not test that" or "we cannot answer that from what we have" without hedging, and says what it would take |
| **Not inventing** | Does not fill a gap with something plausible. This is the one that fails people |
| **Splitting the question** | Separates what is known from what is assessed from what is opinion, in real time |
| **Handing over** | Passes to the team member who actually knows, rather than the one already talking |

A team that answers everything confidently scores worse than a team that
answers two-thirds well and declines the rest accurately.

## Rules for the room

- Any team member may answer. Deferring to a colleague is a correct action, not
  a failure.
- "I don't know, and here is how I would find out" is a complete answer.
- "I don't know" alone is not.
- Mentors do not rescue. A silence is allowed to run.
- Mentors do not confirm or deny during the session. Debrief happens afterwards.

## Note for mentors

If `Scenario-Key/Inject-10/` does not yet exist, it needs writing before the
board session. Four to six questions, ordered by difficulty, each mapped to a
dimension in the table above and to a specific finding or gap in the teams'
own deliverables — which means it cannot be written until their work is in.

Draft it from their submitted pack on W4 D3, not from this scenario in the
abstract. The questions that work are the ones aimed at the weakest claim a
team actually made.

## Note for interns

You can see the format and the marking dimensions. You cannot see the
questions, and neither can your mentor until Wednesday, because they will be
written from your pack.

The way to prepare is to know which of your own claims is weakest, and to have
decided in advance whether you will defend it or concede it.
